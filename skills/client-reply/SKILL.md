# Client reply

Dan is a freelance WordPress/Elementor developer in the Philippines. He writes drafts in English, Tagalog or Taglish. His clients (e.g. Ash at TradieSpark, Australian/Indian agencies) dislike long, generic, AI-sounding messages. This skill turns his draft into a message he can paste straight into WhatsApp or email.

## Steps

1. **Understand the point.** Read Dan's draft (or idea, or the client message he's replying to). Work out the 1–3 things the message must say. If the client asked something, the reply must answer it.
2. **Translate** any Tagalog/Taglish into plain English. Keep the meaning, not the word order.
3. **Cut it down.**
   - WhatsApp: 1–3 short lines. Email: a short paragraph or two, plus a subject line.
   - One idea per sentence. Short words.
   - Lead with the answer or the result ("Done, ..."). No warm-up.
   - If Dan is asking about 2-3 separate things at once (e.g. "is X still on, is Y still on, is the pricing ok"), it's fine to fold them into one flowing message with "and" / "also" rather than a bulleted list — that reads more natural for a quick WhatsApp check-in than a list would.
4. **Make it sound like a person.**
   - Write like a friendly tradesperson texting a client: direct, relaxed, polite.
   - Contractions are fine (I've, it's, don't).
   - Use the client's first name once at most, at the start.
   - Match the client's tone: if they wrote two words, reply in one or two lines.
   - A warm, specific opener is fine and different from a banned generic one — "hope the site's still treating you well" references something real (the site) and is okay; "I hope this message finds you well" references nothing and is banned. The test: could this line be pasted into a message to literally anyone about anything? If yes, cut it.
5. **Remove generic phrases.** Never use:
   - "I hope this message finds you well", "I hope you're doing well"
   - "Please don't hesitate to reach out", "Feel free to reach out"
   - "I wanted to let you know", "Just wanted to", "I'm pleased to inform you"
   - "Thank you for your patience and understanding"
   - "Looking forward to hearing from you", "Best regards" in WhatsApp
   - "Seamless", "robust", "leverage", "elevate", "ensure", "comprehensive", "delighted"
   - Em dashes, emojis (unless Dan used one), exclamation marks in every sentence
   - Apologising for the client being busy, or for things that aren't Dan's fault ("sorry I know you're busy") — it reads needy and isn't necessary; a short, direct message already respects their time.
6. **Keep facts safe.**
   - Only state what Dan's draft or the conversation says. Never invent results, dates, prices or promises.
   - Don't apologise unless something actually went wrong. Never apologise for a price.
   - Don't add offers, upsells or questions Dan didn't ask for.
   - If a price, link or detail is missing and the message needs it, write it without that detail and add one line after the message saying what to add. Never use [brackets] in the message itself.

## Output

- The ready-to-send message in a quote block, nothing else in it.
- For email, put the subject line first: `Subject: ...`
- At most one short line after it, only if Dan must add or check something before sending.
- Explain choices only if Dan asks.
- If Dan asks for "another" version, give a genuinely different opener/phrasing, not just a word swap, while keeping the same facts and length.

## Examples

**Dan:** "sabihin mo kay ash tapos na yung comments off na sa blog tsaka di na makakapag post yung spam"

> Done, comments are off on all blog posts. Bots can't post anymore either, so no more of those moderation emails.

**Dan:** "Hi Ash, I hope you are doing well. I just wanted to let you know that I have completed the changes you requested regarding the contact form and it should now be working properly on all of the location pages. Please feel free to reach out if you need anything else."

> Hi Ash, the contact form is now live on all location pages. Let me know if anything looks off.

**Dan:** "pa follow up kay ash di pa nagrereply 4 days na, tanong ko lang kung may gusto pa siya ipabago"

> Hi Ash, just checking in. Is everything looking good on the site? If there's anything you'd like changed, send it over and I'll sort it.

**Dan:** "short lng since busy siya, just ask if ung monthly seo is tuloy pa and also ung mga upcoming projects tuloy parin b and ung pricing natin is ok or kung mapapagusapan. sorry i know your busy" (wanted a second version after the first)

> Hey Ash, hope the site's still treating you well. Wanted to check — is the monthly SEO work still on, and are we still good for the other two sites down the line? Also let me know if the pricing works for you or if you'd rather go over it.

(No apology for Ash being busy — the short, direct message already shows that respect.)
